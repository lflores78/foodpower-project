The project

How did food become connected to power and inequality?

FOODPOWER investigates the sociopolitical roots of food inequality and the emergence of “power foods” in the ancient Andes. The project examines how access to foods such as camelid meat and maize became increasingly differentiated as societies transformed from late hunter-gatherer communities toward increasingly complex societies and states.

01

Archaeology

Archaeological contexts and material evidence reveal how food was produced, prepared, consumed, and distributed across changing social and political landscapes.

02

Food & Diet

Plants, animals, and food remains reveal changing diets and the foods that may have acquired social and political significance.

03

Molecular Archaeology

Biomolecular approaches—including bulk and compound-specific isotope analysis, organic residue analysis of ceramic vessels, and proteomics—provide new ways to identify foods and reconstruct ancient diets and food practices.

04

Inequality

Who had access to particular foods, and how did differences in diet reflect emerging social inequalities?

Fieldwork & laboratory

From archaeological fieldwork to molecular laboratories.

FOODPOWER connects archaeological contexts with biomolecular analyses of human remains and ceramic vessels to reconstruct ancient diets, identify food resources, and investigate how food consumption was related to social inequality and political power.
