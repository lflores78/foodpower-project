The project

How did food become connected to power and inequality?

FOODPOWER investigates changing foodways from the later hunter-gatherer period through the development of farming and increasingly complex societies across the Andes. The project combines archaeological excavation with archaeobotany, zooarchaeology, material culture studies, stable isotopes, organic residue analysis, and other approaches in molecular archaeology.

01

Archaeology

Excavations and archaeological evidence reveal how people produced, prepared, consumed, and distributed food.

02

Food & Diet

Plants, animals, food remains, and dietary evidence help reconstruct changing foodways through time.

03

Molecular Archaeology

Biomolecular methods provide new ways to identify foods and reconstruct ancient diets and lifeways.

04

Inequality

Who had access to particular foods, and how did differences in diet relate to emerging social inequalities?

Fieldwork & laboratory

From excavation trenches to molecular laboratories.

FOODPOWER connects archaeological contexts in the Andes with laboratory analyses designed to identify foods, reconstruct diets, and investigate the social dimensions of food consumption.
